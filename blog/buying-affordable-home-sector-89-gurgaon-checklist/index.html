Before you start: know what “affordable” means for you

Sector 89 sits on the Dwarka Expressway side of Gurgaon, and it has a mix of ready homes, under-construction projects and resale flats. That variety is good news for first-time buyers, but it also means two flats that look similar online can be very different once you check the paperwork, the builder and the real monthly cost.

This checklist walks you through the steps in a simple order. Use it whether you work with a consultant (including us) or on your own. We have deliberately not quoted prices or market numbers here, because they change. Wherever figures matter, check current rates from official sources or ask for them in writing.

Start with your own definition of affordable. For most families it is not just the flat price. Write down:

  • Your total budget, including stamp duty, registration charges, legal fees, brokerage (if any), society or maintenance deposits, and moving and furnishing costs.
  • Your comfortable monthly EMI, after household expenses and savings. Many lenders will approve more than you are comfortable paying, so decide this number first.
  • Your must-haves: number of bedrooms, floor preference, school or office distance, parking, power backup and lift needs for elderly family members.

Understand the type of project you are looking at

In Gurgaon you will hear terms such as “affordable housing”, “Deen Dayal Jan Awas Yojana (DDJAY)” and regular group housing. These come under different Haryana government policies, with different rules on allotment, payment plans, unit sizes and resale. A flat sold under a government affordable housing scheme may, for example, be allotted through a draw and may have conditions on when and how it can be resold.

Ask the seller clearly which policy the project falls under, and then read the current policy rules on the Haryana Town and Country Planning Department (DTCP) website yourself. Rules are updated from time to time, so do not rely on old blog posts or forwarded messages.

Check the project on the HARERA Gurugram website

This is the single most useful step, and it is free. Under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, most new residential projects must be registered with the state authority. In Gurgaon that is the Haryana Real Estate Regulatory Authority, Gurugram (HARERA Gurugram).

  1. Get the RERA registration number from the builder or seller. It should also appear on the project’s advertisements.
  2. Search it on the official HARERA Gurugram website and confirm the project name, promoter name and address match what you were told.
  3. Look at the registered completion date and any extensions. Compare it with the possession date in the sales pitch.
  4. Open the quarterly progress updates and approved plans if they are available, to see how construction is really going.
  5. Check for complaints or orders against the project or promoter. A few complaints are common in large projects; a pattern of unresolved ones is a warning sign.
  6. Check your agent too. Real estate agents should also be registered with HARERA. You can verify any agent’s registration number on the same website.

Tip: Take screenshots of what you find, with the date. If anything changes later, you will have a record of what was shown when you decided.

Documents to ask for and verify

Ask for copies before you pay any booking amount, and have a property lawyer review them. The exact list depends on whether you are buying new or resale, but this is a sensible starting point.

For a new or under-construction flat

  • RERA registration certificate and the registered project details
  • Licence and approved building plans from the relevant authority
  • Title documents for the land, and confirmation that the land is not under dispute
  • Environmental and other clearances that apply to the project
  • The draft Builder-Buyer Agreement (BBA) or agreement for sale, so you can read the payment plan, possession clause, delay penalties and cancellation terms
  • Details of any bank that has approved the project for home loans

For a resale (ready) flat

  • The registered sale deed or conveyance deed in the seller’s name, and the chain of earlier documents
  • Occupation Certificate (OC) for the building
  • Latest property tax, electricity, water and maintenance receipts, with no pending dues
  • A No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the society or maintenance agency
  • If the seller has a home loan, a letter from their bank on the outstanding amount and the process to release the original documents
  • An encumbrance check, to confirm the flat is not mortgaged or tied up in a legal case

Keep the name, flat number and area consistent across every document. Small mismatches are often harmless, but they should be explained and corrected before registration, not after.

Plan a proper site visit (or two)

Photos and sample flats show the best version of a project. A real site visit shows the rest. Try to visit once on a weekday and once in the evening or on a weekend.

  • The approach road: Is it paved and lit? How long does it take to reach the main road at peak hours?
  • Construction status: Compare what you see with the progress shown on the HARERA website.
  • Inside the flat: Check light and ventilation, seepage marks, plumbing, electrical points, and the actual carpet area against the agreement.
  • Common areas: Lifts, fire safety equipment, power backup, water supply, parking and security.
  • Daily life: Distance to schools, hospitals, markets, public transport and your workplace.
  • Talk to residents in ready towers, if there are any. Ask about maintenance, water and how the builder or society responds to problems.

Sort out your home loan and the full cost early

Speak to two or three lenders before you book. Ask each for a written estimate of the loan amount, interest rate type, processing fees and the documents they need. Interest rates and loan rules change, so check the current figures on the day you apply.

Next, list every cost on top of the flat price and ask the seller to confirm it in writing: preferential location charges, parking, club membership, maintenance deposit, GST (where it applies to under-construction property) and anything else in the price sheet. Then add stamp duty and registration charges at the current Haryana rates, which you can confirm with the sub-registrar office or the official revenue department portal.

If the total goes above your budget, it is better to know now than after you have paid a booking amount.

From booking to registration: protect yourself

  • Pay only through traceable channels (bank transfer or cheque) to the account named in the agreement, and always take a receipt.
  • Read the agreement fully before signing, especially possession dates, penalty clauses, cancellation and refund terms, and the exact unit area.
  • Do not sign blank or incomplete papers, and keep copies of everything you sign.
  • Register the sale deed at the sub-registrar office as required by law, and make sure the final deed matches what you agreed.
  • After registration, update the property in your name for electricity, water, property tax and the society records.

Your quick checklist

  1. Total budget and comfortable EMI written down
  2. Project type and policy confirmed (affordable scheme or regular project)
  3. RERA number verified on the HARERA Gurugram website
  4. Agent’s HARERA registration verified
  5. Documents collected and reviewed by a lawyer
  6. Two site visits done at different times
  7. Loan quotes from more than one lender
  8. All extra costs confirmed in writing
  9. Payments made only to the account in the agreement
  10. Sale deed registered and utilities transferred
